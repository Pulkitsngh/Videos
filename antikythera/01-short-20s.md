## Facts used (accurate wording)
- **Discovery:**
  - Greek sponge divers found the shipwreck in **1900**.
  - Objects were recovered in **1900–1901**.
  - The gear fragment was noticed in **1902** (by Valerios Stais).
  - "1901 में… मिला" is the commonly used date for the recovery.
- **Age:** usually dated **2nd–1st century BCE** (about 2,000+ years old).
- **Gears:**
  - About **30 surviving bronze gears**.
  - X-ray CT scans (2005 onwards) revealed the inner gears and inscriptions.
- **Function:**
  - Modelled the **positions of the Sun and Moon**, **lunar phases** and **eclipse cycles**.
  - Also showed the **Olympic Games** calendar.
  - Widely called the **world's oldest known analogue computer**.
- **"1,000 years ahead":** devices of comparable gear complexity aren't known until **medieval astronomical clocks**, more than a millennium later.
- **Now in:** National Archaeological Museum, Athens.
