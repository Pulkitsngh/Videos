## Why the first 4 seconds hold viewers
1. **The number plus the impossibility:** "600 years old" + "nobody can read it". Two hooks in one line.
2. **The real page on screen from frame 1.** The alien-looking script is the scroll-stopper, and it's genuine.
3. **No name until 0:13.** Curiosity carries viewers through the middle.
4. **It ends on a question** ("गुप्त ज्ञान या धोखा?"). That drives comments, which push the Short to more viewers.

## Facts used (wording kept accurate)
- The vellum is **radiocarbon-dated to the early 15th century (about 1404–1438)**. "600 साल पुरानी" is fair.
- It's written in an **unknown script**, and **no decipherment is accepted** by scholars, despite many claims. "कोई नहीं पढ़ पाया" means no one has convincingly read it.
- It has illustrations of **unidentified plants**, **astronomical/zodiac diagrams** and "bathing" scenes. Avoid the nude figures in the video.
- Famous **WWI/WWII-era cryptographers** (e.g. **William Friedman**) studied it without success.
- It's named after the book dealer **Wilfrid Voynich**, who acquired it in **1912**.
- It's kept at the **Beinecke Library, Yale (MS 408)**. The Beinecke offers **free high-resolution scans**, and many are on Wikimedia Commons as **public domain**. These are perfect real visuals.
- "Hoax" is presented as **one theory**, not fact.
