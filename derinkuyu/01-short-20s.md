## Why the opening holds viewers
1. **A hard fact in the first second** ("1963…"): it sounds like real news, not a gimmick.
2. **An everyday act with an impossible result:** "broke a wall in his house" leads to "a whole city". Anyone can picture it.
3. **A realistic first frame:** a hammer through a wall, dust and a dark hole. It feels like real footage, not fantasy.
4. **A new number every 4 s** (1963 → 85 m → 20,000 → 500 kg) keeps the "wow" coming with no dead moment.

## Facts used (accurate wording)
- **Rediscovered in 1963** during a house renovation in Derinkuyu, when a resident broke through a wall and found a passage. Opened to visitors in **1969**.
- **Depth:** reaches about **85 m**, across many levels; only part is open to tourists.
- **Capacity:** often estimated at **up to ~20,000 people** with their livestock and food stores. Present this as an estimate ("छिप सकते थे").
- **Rolling stone doors:** large round stones about 1–1.5 m tall, weighing up to **about 500 kg**, could be rolled shut from inside.
- **Inside:** ventilation shafts, wells, stables, storage rooms, wine and oil presses, a chapel. It is linked by a tunnel to the **Kaymaklı** underground city, several km away.
- **Origin:** early digging is often attributed to the **Phrygians (around the 8th–7th century BCE)**. It was greatly expanded in the **Byzantine era**, when people used it to shelter from raids. Avoid giving one exact founding date.
- It is part of the **UNESCO World Heritage Site "Göreme National Park and the Rock Sites of Cappadocia"**.
